Every year, when the new Horizon work programmes drop, I spend a few hours scrolling through calls like someone discovering a new menu at a favorite restaurant. It's energizing, honestly. The breadth of challenges is humbling, but it's also overwhelming: there are hundreds of calls, and most of us don't have time to parse through all of them.

So I've done that work for you. Below is a personal, curated selection of calls where I genuinely see opportunity and more importantly, where I know I can contribute. This isn't meant to be comprehensive or definitive. It's a navigator's view: here's what caught my attention, why it matters, and how I might fit in if you're assembling a team.

TLDR: The EU Commission just dropped the grants description for the https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/funding/funding-opportunities/funding-programmes-and-open-calls/horizon-europe/horizon-europe-work-programmes_en horizon programme 2026-2027. CLUSTER 1: AGRICULTURE & SUSTAINABILITY

This is where I spend most of my energy. The calls here reflect a real shift: from "reduce harm" to "actively regenerate." That's exciting.

Three calls stand out. First, there's the focus on carbon farming and zero pollution (HORIZON-CL6-2027-02-CLIMATE-03, HORIZON-CL6-2027-01-ZEROPOLLUTION-03). Both are asking research teams to scale up regenerative practices and build the monitoring infrastructure to prove they work. Second, rural competitiveness through innovation (HORIZON-CL6-2026-02-COMMUNITIES-01) — less glamorous than carbon farming, but critical. It's about helping farmers and foresters actually adopt sustainable practices at scale, which means data, training, and participatory decision-making. This is exactly what I spent the last two years doing at Digit Soil, working with 200+ farms across Switzerland to measure soil health and carbon potential.

Why it matters: Agriculture contributes more than 10% of the EU's total greenhouse gas emissions and the sector occupies 38% of the EU's land area. Scale matters. Without serious innovation and data-driven monitoring, climate targets don't happen.

What I can provide: Deep knowledge of regenerative agriculture and soil resilience. Environmental monitoring and geospatial modelling to track land-use change. Data pipelines and infrastructure for carbon certification and impact measurement.

CLUSTER 2: DATA & AI FOR SUSTAINABILITY

Here's where I think the real infrastructure challenge sits. The calls here are about building the foundational layer that unlocks all the others.

Earth Intelligence solutions using environmental observations (open topic under the Green Deal) and environmental observations and digital solutions in support of the Green Deal (HORIZON-CL6-2026-03-GOVERNANCE-06, HORIZON-CL6-2026-03-GOVERNANCE-07) are asking: how do we unlock the potential of environmental data? How do we integrate satellite imagery, sensor networks, and in-situ observations into systems that teams can actually use for decision-making?

The calls recognize something fundamental: we're drowning in environmental data but starving for insight. Terabytes of satellite imagery exist. Sensor networks are proliferating. But most organizations lack the data infrastructure, the ML pipelines, and the tools to turn that raw feed into actionable intelligence. Without this foundational layer, all the domain expertise in the world (regenerative ag, conservation, climate adaptation) remains constrained by data bottlenecks.

What I can provide: Data engineering and infrastructure to unlock environmental datasets at scale. ETL pipelines and cloud architecture for processing geospatial and environmental data. Building the foundational systems that enable domain experts to do their best work.

CLUSTER 3: SUSTAINABLE CITIES

Cities are complex systems, and making them more resilient and sustainable requires understanding what's actually happening on the ground. These calls recognize that data and participatory planning are two sides of the same coin.

Resilient, inclusive, healthy and green rural, coastal and urban communities (HORIZON-CL6-2026-02-COMMUNITIES-01) and innovative governance, environmental observations and digital solutions in support of the Green Deal (HORIZON-CL6-2027-03-GOVERNANCE-04) aim at making cities and communities more adaptive and combining real-time environmental monitoring with genuine community input to design better urban spaces.

This is where data infrastructure meets participatory governance. You need the sensors and dashboards to understand urban heat, air quality, water systems, and green space dynamics. But you also need frameworks to bring residents, planners, and civil society into decision-making. Neither works without the other. I'm excited about this angle because it's where my experience from Berner Fachhochschule — leading participatory research and stakeholder engagement in land management — directly intersects with modern data systems. I'm actually collaborating with Urban Data Lab on projects in this space, and the combination of rigorous data + real community voice is genuinely where innovation happens.

Why it matters: Cities concentrate people, infrastructure, and climate risks. Heat islands, flooding, and air pollution aren't just environmental problems—they're equity problems. But cities also concentrate data streams and decision-making power.

What I can provide: Participatory planning and data-driven urban monitoring frameworks. Helping teams translate environmental data into actionable insights for city planning. Building systems that serve both technical experts and community stakeholders.

CONCLUSION

What excites me about this year's calls is the shift they represent. The EU is finally treating data infrastructure, participatory governance, and ecological resilience as interconnected challenges. Carbon accounting matters only if you can measure it. Sustainable cities only happen if communities shape them. Regenerative agriculture only scales with the right monitoring and support systems.

These calls reflect real funding appetite for the work that actually bridges those worlds: the data engineering, the geospatial modelling, the dissemination infrastructure, the participatory planning frameworks.
